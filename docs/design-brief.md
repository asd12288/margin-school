# Margin School — design brief

Written to be handed to a designer. It describes intent and feel, not implementation.

## What it is

A subscription platform that teaches people the financial markets from the very beginning. One monthly price, everything included. We produce all the courses ourselves — there are no third-party instructors and nothing is sold per course.

Launching in **French and English**.

## Who it is for

**A complete beginner.** Someone curious about markets who has never placed a trade, doesn't know what a candlestick is, and is quietly intimidated by the whole subject. They are an adult — often someone with a job and some savings who feels they *should* understand this and doesn't.

They are not a trader. They are not technical. They have been put off before by websites that assumed knowledge they didn't have.

## The design problem, in one sentence

> It must feel credible enough to trust with questions about money, and calm enough not to frighten someone who knows nothing.

Those pull in opposite directions, and the tension is the interesting part of this brief. Too serious and it intimidates. Too friendly and nobody believes it.

## How it should feel

- **Calm.** The subject is already stressful. The interface should lower the heart rate, not raise it. Space, air, room to breathe.
- **Clear and current.** It should look like a well-made modern product: one sans-serif family, cool neutrals, a vibrant brand colour used sparingly and with intent. People read here for long stretches, so the reading column and the type scale get real attention — but the reference is good software, not a printed book.
- **Quietly confident.** It knows what it's talking about and doesn't need to shout. No urgency, no persuasion tactics.
- **Encouraging, not congratulatory.** Progress should feel earned and visible. Never patronising, never confetti.
- **Orderly.** A beginner's core fear is being lost. Always obvious where you are, what's next, and how much is left.

## What it must not feel like

- **A trading terminal.** Dense panels, blinking numbers, walls of red and green. This is the single strongest thing to design away from — it's what makes beginners close the tab.
- **Crypto hype.** Neon, dark gradients, aggressive geometry, anything implying easy money. We teach risk; the design has to be honest about that.
- **A children's app.** The mechanics borrow from good learning products — streaks, progress, review — but the tone is adult. Money is serious.
- **A bank.** Corporate, cold, committee-designed. Trustworthy without being institutional.

## Things that will shape the design

- **Charts live inside lessons.** Real market charts sit in the middle of editorial content. They must feel like part of the page, not an embedded widget from another product. This is the hardest thing to get right, and probably where the design identity gets decided.
- **Long-form reading.** Lessons are text, diagrams, examples and exercises. Reading comfort outranks visual flourish.
- **Progress is everywhere.** Where you are in a lesson, a chapter, a course, an entire path. It needs a visual language that repeats at several scales without becoming noise.
- **An AI tutor sits alongside the lesson** in a later phase. Leave room for a companion surface that feels like part of the page rather than a bolted-on chat bubble.
- **Light and dark.** People study in the evening. Both are first-class, not one plus an afterthought.
- **French and English.** French runs roughly 15–20% longer than English. Layouts must survive text expansion without breaking.
- **Locked states are constant.** A subscription product shows "this is included, subscribe to continue" a great deal. It deserves real design, not a dead end.

## What we are not asking for

No pricing psychology — no countdown timers, struck-through prices, or scarcity. It's one subscription, and the honest argument is depth and quality.

No instructor faces, testimonials, ratings or student counts. We don't use them, and we won't invent them.

## What would help most

A direction, not a full system: typography, spacing, colour temperature, the shape of a lesson page, and how a chart sits inside one. If a lesson page feels right, the rest of the product follows from it.

## Status

**Superseded in part.** The direction was built in Phase 2 and is documented in [design-system.md](design-system.md), which is now the source of truth for typography, colour and motion. The intent above still holds — calm, orderly, no hype, no invented people — but the visual answer landed as a modern product interface rather than the editorial, serif-led direction this brief originally implied. The "How it should feel" section has been corrected to match.
